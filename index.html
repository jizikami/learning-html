A lighthouse is a lamp, a lens and a promise: that on the worst night of the year there will still be one light on, in one known place, saying the same thing it said last night. This site is about the light, the people who kept it, and the long hours in between — in sixteen quotes, five ideas, one drawn tower, and one machine that refuses to stay switched on.

One night on the watch

21:00 to 02:00, roughly

Light does not keep itself. Before electricity, a keeper's night had four fixed points in it, and they came round again every night for years.

  1. 21:00

    Light up

    Carry the lamp up the stairs, trim the wick, set it behind the lens and write the hour in the log. From here until dawn, the light does not go out for a visitor, for a meal, or for a ship that is already safe.

  2. 22:30

    Watch the flame

    A bad wick smokes, and smoke on the glass turns a beam into a blur. Every four hours: trim, polish the lens with a soft cloth, and look at the flame through the dark until your eyes stop watering.

  3. 00:00

    Wind the clock

    The lens floats on mercury and is turned by a clockwork weight. Let it run down and the light stops flashing — a still light in a place where sailors expect a flash is worse than no light at all.

  4. 02:00

    Write it down

    Weather, wind, visibility, every ship that passed, every strange thing on the water. The log is the part of the night that survives it. Nothing dramatic ever happens in a good log. That is the point.

Every light has a name

At night a sailor cannot see which tower is which — so the tower tells them with rhythm. On a chart it is written in a code that takes one line, for example Fl(3) W 15s: three white flashes, then a pause, and the whole pattern repeats every fifteen seconds. Count the flashes, read the interval, and you know exactly where you are.

  • Fixed — a steady light. The oldest kind, and the easiest to mistake for a star.
  • Flashing — dark for longer than it is bright, at a fixed rhythm. One flash, one identity.
  • Group flashing — two, three or four flashes together and then a long darkness. The group is the fingerprint.
  • Occulting — the mirror image of flashing: mostly lit, briefly dark.
  • Isophase — light and dark of exactly equal length, which is much harder to fake from the deck of a moving boat.

Every one of those patterns is a timer, and a timer is about the easiest thing in the world to write. That is why the light got its own entry in the projects list: it is a rhythm, and a rhythm can be coded.
