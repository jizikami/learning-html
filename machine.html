You turn it on. It turns itself off.

A lighthouse keeper's whole job is to keep one lamp lit. So here is the opposite: a box with a switch on the lid, a lamp on the front, and a hand that comes out of the side to push the switch back down. It has one job, and it is very serious about it.

The game

Don't press it

A keeper's real skill was sitting still next to a thing they were not allowed to touch. Press once to light the lamp, then keep your hands off it for fifteen seconds. Touch it once and you begin again. It will shake, change its label and ask you to look at it. Ignore it.

Why this machine exists

Somebody really did describe one. In the 1950s Marvin Minsky imagined a machine whose only function was to switch itself off; a version of it ended up in AI lectures, and later on desks all over the world as a wooden box with a lid and a hand. It is funny for exactly one reason: the machine wins, every single time, and it never gets bored.

It is also a nice lesson in state. There are only five things this box can be doing — off, on, reaching, pushing back, retracting — and if those five are kept in order, the toy can never get stuck. Get the order wrong once and you get a lamp that stays on, or a hand that never goes home.
